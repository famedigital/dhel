Process · inbound

KTM → Paro · how the morning works

Monday 14 September 2026 · KB401 · plan backward from 09:10 departure

Tribhuvan Intl · Terminal I

~3 hours before · aim 06:00–06:15

~45 min before departure

  1. Night before in Kathmandu. Pack cabin: passport, visas PDF, phones, chargers, light jacket, any medicines. Keep one change of clothes in cabin in case bags are delayed.
  2. ~05:30–06:00 hotel departure. Allow KTM traffic. Domestic/regional flights use Terminal I — follow Drukair / Royal Bhutan Airlines signs.
  3. Check-in & bag drop. Present passport + e-ticket. Confirm all four names match passports exactly. Request window seats (left side outbound from KTM often better for peaks).
  4. Security & immigration (Nepal exit). Keep boarding pass and passport together. Liquids in cabin must meet airline rules.
  5. Boarding. Flight time short — use the toilet before boarding. Cameras ready for Himalayan views if clear; clouds are common in September.
  6. Descent into Paro. Spectacular valley approach. Seatbelts on; cabin crew may ask window shades open for landing.
  7. Paro immigration. Queue with group. Hand passport + visa clearance printout. Smile, no photography inside hall.
  8. Baggage & customs. Collect bags · green channel if nothing to declare. Exit to arrivals — look for Silverpine Tours board with your names.
  9. Meet guide & driver. SIM / WhatsApp check · short briefing · load bags into Hiace/H1 · drive to Thimphu (~1.5 hrs · stops if you want photos).

Places · Thimphu

Places & context

Thimphu · what you will see

Capital valley · no traffic lights · day 08 focus

Viewpoint · monument

Buddha Dordenma (Buddha Point)

A great seated Buddha overlooking Thimphu valley — one of the largest bronze Buddhas in the world. Inside the base: thousands of smaller Buddha statues. Guide explains consecration story briefly; you walk the terrace for photographs. Modest dress · remove hats · no climbing on structures.

Allow 45–75 minutes including drive up Kuenselphodrang hill. Best light mid-morning or late afternoon.

Fortress · seat of government

Tashichho Dzong

“Fortress of the glorious religion” — summer seat of government and monastic body. White walls, gold roofs, courtyard. Access to interiors follows temple rules and timing; guide manages permissions. Photography often restricted inside chapels — ask before shooting.

Combine with a short stroll near the river if energy allows. Security may check bags at entrance.

Town · optional

Centenary Farmers Market & craft

Weekend market vibe midweek is lighter. Chillis, cheese, dried fish, textiles. Good for small gifts without rushing. Norzin Lam craft stalls nearby for stamps, scarves, simple souvenirs.

Cash BTN or small USD. Bargain gently. We skip long shopping stops unless you ask.

Places · Dochula & Punakha

Places & context

Pass · rivers · fortress

Day 09 spine · Thimphu to Punakha valley

Mountain pass · ~3,100 m

Dochula Pass · 108 Druk Wangyal Chortens

Memorial chortens for Bhutanese soldiers · prayer flags · on clear days a panorama of Himalayan peaks. September can be hazy or cloudy — still worth the stop for the chorten field and tea if the café is open.

Walk slowly · thin air. 20–40 minutes. Toilets available at tourist facilities. Jacket recommended.

Icon · confluence of Pho Chhu & Mo Chhu

Punakha Dzong

Arguably Bhutan’s most beautiful dzong — whitewashed walls between two rivers, jacaranda season in spring (September is greener). Built by Zhabdrung Ngawang Namgyal. Guide covers founding story, courtyard etiquette, and which chapels are open that day.

Cross the cantilevered wooden bridge · remove hats · walk clockwise in courtyards · no shorts in temples. Allow 1–1.5 hours. Shoes off in chapels — wear socks you can walk in.

Optional if time / energy

Chimi Lhakhang (Fertility temple)

Short walk through rice fields to Drukpa Kunley’s temple — colourful, informal, popular with travellers. Include only if the group wants; otherwise protect rest before Paro transfer next day.

Places · Paro

Places & context

Paro valley · fortress & Tiger’s Nest

Days 10–11 · climax of the west circuit

Fortress · National Museum nearby

Paro Rinpung Dzong

Watchtower fortress above the Paro Chhu · classic approach via wooden bridge and flagstone path. Courtyard architecture · monastic life · views over the valley floor and airport approach path.

Usually 45–75 minutes. Good afternoon activity after the Punakha–Paro drive. Museum optional if open and energy remains.

Hike · UNESCO-famous cliff monastery

Paro Taktsang · Tiger’s Nest

Guru Rinpoche legend · monastery clinging to a cliff ~900 m above the valley floor. Round trip typically 3–5 hours depending on pace. Trail: forest switchbacks → cafeteria viewpoint → steeper final section with steps and a waterfall bridge → monastery complex.

Fitness: manageable without elite training if you pace and hydrate. Stop often. Pony: optional cash hire to cafeteria level (not all the way to the monastery) — book via guide morning-of. Gear: broken-in shoes, layers, rain shell, sun hat, 1–2 L water, light snacks. Etiquette: no drones · modest clothes · cameras restricted in some chapels · silence near shrines.

Start early to avoid heat and crowds. Guide carries entry permits. Descend carefully — knees matter more than ascent.
